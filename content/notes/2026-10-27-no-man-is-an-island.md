Alex Borretti argues that sustained intellectual work depends on a community: people need a shared body of work to build on, and peers who give their contributions consequence and recognition. The essay traces this through software engineering, where AI can write code and answer questions, making human writing, open-source work, and learning feel less consequential. It extends the concern to mathematics and intellectual life more broadly.

The worry is not only lost traffic or status. If people stop contributing to a common project, the community itself thins out, reducing both the material others can build on and the social motivation that helps sustain long-term work. Borretti argues that intrinsic motivation alone is too fleeting to reliably replace that social fabric. This is a thesis about a possible cultural effect of AI, not evidence that these communities have already disappeared.
